Back-to-Back Duathlon Weekends

One week after Guelph, it was time to race again in Welland.

Coming into this event, the main question was simple: after Guelph, could I still perform and still be competitive? Racing hard on consecutive weekends is always a good way to find out what is really there and what is still missing.

Guelph had already taken a fair bit out of me. It was hilly, hard, and important from a qualification point of view. Welland looked flatter and more straightforward on paper, but that did not automatically mean easier. On a flatter course there is less hiding. If the legs are not there, it shows.

The Qualification Picture

Like Guelph, this race mattered for age-group qualification.

The goal is still the same: win the age group, or at least finish close enough to give myself a realistic chance of qualifying for the World Championships. That is what gives these races a different feel. It is not just about placing well on the day. It is also about who is in the actual five-year age-group qualification category, who has submitted an expression of interest, and who will still matter once Triathlon Canada starts allocating spots.

That uncertainty is part of the game. You can race well, finish on the podium, and still walk away not really knowing what it means yet.

Race Morning

I arrived early, got set up without any rush, and had time to look around and see a few familiar faces. What I did not see much of were calves marked 40-49, which did not make the qualification picture any easier to read.

The morning was calm for me, but transition itself had a bit of chaos. One athlete, clearly at their first race, turned up with a mountain bike and seemed entirely unconcerned by the fact that they were not even racking it properly, just leaning it against other athletes’ racked bikes. That led to a fair amount of discussion with the referees before things were sorted out.

Small details like that do not decide the race, but they definitely shape the feel of the morning.

A Different Kind of Course

The format here was 5/20/2.5, which is the standard way to organise a sprint duathlon. Even so, it is still strange how every race seems to do this a bit differently. In Guelph the format was 2/20/5, which changes the race quite a lot, and not just because of the missing 500 metres. A long first run and a short second run reward a very different type of pacing and a different type of patience.

On paper, Welland looked like a course that should suit me better: flatter, more honest, and more about sustained effort than repeated changes of rhythm. That can be good news, but it also means you have to be ready to do the work. There is less terrain to save you.

Run 1

I started the first run trying to stay under control. That part went reasonably well, but even so I faded quite a bit toward the end. That was when I saw two athletes with 40-49 on their calves come past me.

I was still close enough to them that the race did not feel gone, but it was clear I was in for a fight if I wanted to finish at least third. That was also assuming there was not someone else in the category even further ahead that I had simply not seen.

Bike

As always, I struggled a bit getting the left shoe on after the mount line. Not as badly as in Guelph, but still not clean. That was exactly when one of the two 40-49 athletes who had passed me on the run came past me again on the bike. So I had actually done the transition better overall, but the messy mount still cost me the position again.

That only lasted about 500 metres. At the first turnaround he was right in front of me and had to stop with a puncture. Brutally unlucky. I would have been furious. We will never know whether I would have beaten him fairly in the race, but on this day that was the end of his race.

After that I settled in on the bike and rode within myself. This is clearly my strongest discipline at the moment. A few riders passed me, but none were in my age group. Most of them were on fancy TT bikes as well, which does make me want one.

At around kilometre 3, the other 40-49 athlete who had passed me earlier on the run came by as well. He was putting in a strong ride. I stayed with him until around kilometre 5, where there was a rise. I attacked there, and that was enough to break him. By the end of the bike I had put roughly two minutes into him.

Run 2

My main focus on the second run was simply to stay strong enough not to get passed again. I knew the guy I had dropped on the bike was probably a bit faster than me on the run, but I did not know exactly how far ahead I was or whether it was enough to hold him off.

This was not a fun run. I started to struggle, with fatigue and maybe also the heat, and the pace dropped badly. At the same time I could not see anyone with 40-49 on their calves ahead of me, so I never had the visual target that might have helped me push harder. I was hoping I was racing for first, but in practice I was running defensively, trying at the very least not to let anyone come back to me.

About 20 metres from the finish line I heard someone sprinting behind me. Not knowing who it was, I had no choice but to sprint as well in case it was the athlete from my category finally catching me. I managed to hold him off, only to find out afterwards that he was in the 20-29 category, so it would not have changed anything in my own race.

Result and What It Means

The final result was third in category. That is a podium, which is never a bad thing, but I was still a bit disappointed. Realistically, this was not the best possible outcome for qualification chances. At the same time, it was a fair defeat. The two athletes ahead of me were simply too strong on the day.

What happens next depends on details that are still outside my control: the exact age of everyone next year and who submitted their EOI. As usual, we now wait for the federation to do its homework and tell everyone where things actually stand.

Final Thoughts

Overall, mixed feelings. Third place is still a podium finish, and backing up Guelph with another competitive race one week later is not nothing. But it was also obvious that my run is still lagging behind where it needs to be. There is work to do there. I probably also need to lose a bit of weight. The bike is another possible limiter, although that is not something I can really solve immediately.

There is still one more proper chance to qualify cleanly: Barrie on August 9. On paper that is another course that should suit me well because it is flat. At the same time, it is also a competitive race, so there will be no easy way through. Looking at last year’s results, I could have won there, but last year it was not a qualifying event. This year it is, which will likely make it a very different race.
