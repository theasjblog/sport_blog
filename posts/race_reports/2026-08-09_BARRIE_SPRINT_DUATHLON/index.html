Barrie Sprint Duathlon

A return race after an ER visit and a build-up that made just getting to the start line feel uncertain.
race_reports
duathlon
Author

Adrian Joseph

Published

August 9, 2026

Pre-race

This felt like a return race more than anything else.

Three weeks earlier I was in ER with chest pain, and three days earlier I started blood pressure medication. For a while it was not even clear whether I would make it to this start -or the finish- line at all.

The doctor said that, even if there was no sign of heart issues, it was probably a good idea to skip this one given how recently I had started the medication. I decided to race anyway. The obvious disclaimer applies here: don’t be like me; if your doctor tells you not to race, listen.

The main reason I was willing to take the risk was that this was a duathlon, not a triathlon. No swim. That matters, because swimming is notoriously where the vast majority of heart-related accidents happen. On the bike or the run, if I had felt anything concerning, I could simply have stopped and gotten assistance on land.

Before even thinking about performance, the first objective was just to get to the line and finish safely.

A Tough Build-up

I knew I was not ready for a good race.

Between the ER visit and the mandatory period of taking things easy afterwards, I had missed exactly the kind of high-end work that gives you that sharp edge you need to be competitive. I was not coming into this race with the feeling that I could really push the top end. At best, I hoped I could manage the effort.

Then, during race week, I found out this event was also the Ontario Provincial Championship for sprint duathlon. That was not ideal news for a supposedly “easy” return race. It meant the field was likely to be deeper and harder than usual.

On top of that, I started taper week feeling completely drained. On Monday my Garmin training readiness was 1/100, which is as low as it goes. That confirmed pretty clearly that the body was not in a great place.

So the priority in the final days was simple: recovery first. I kept things easy, took single easy days seriously, and tried to arrive at the race feeling less depleted than I had at the start of the week. I did manage to recover enough that by race morning I felt functional again, which at that point was a meaningful win on its own.

Race morning

During the warm-up I actually felt OK.

Sure, I was getting out of breath easily, but I was expecting that. Still, I felt reasonably well. Not fitter, but a bit more settled. Given everything that had happened in the previous few weeks, that was enough to give me some confidence that I could at least get through the day.

Run 1

I started the first run with the usual intention of controlling myself, and with the usual outcome of not really controlling myself at all.

The target was around 4:20/km. Instead, I went out at about 4:05/km. That is just classic race-day stupidity from me at this point. Still, early on I felt OK. I was working, but not in a way that immediately felt destructive.

The only athletes I could really see ahead of me at first looked older, which was a slightly mixed feeling. Not great for the overall race, but at least they did not look like direct competition for my age group.

By the second lap, though, the lack of fitness started to come through. The pace drifted out beyond 4:20/km, and that was when a couple of guys from my age group came past me. Not surprising, but still a clear reminder that I was racing without the engine I would normally want for this kind of event.

Bike

Then onto the bike, where of course I immediately messed up the tech.

I forgot to press the transition button, so part of the ride ended up being recorded as transition. Not ideal, but also very on brand.

Still, the actual riding felt good.

I was keeping things under control and riding mostly by feel rather than by chasing power numbers -of course I did not even have power numbers since my watch thought I was still in transition for half the ride…-. The idea was to sit at an effort that felt strong but not reckless, something that would not completely destroy the legs before the final run. For that kind of controlled ride, it actually went fairly well. I was moving through the field and passing people, including one of the athletes in my age group who had gone by me on the first run.

Looking at the data afterwards, it was not an especially fast ride. On a very flat course like this, I know I can ride faster on a better day. But given the interrupted build-up, the medication change, and the basic goal of just getting through the race safely, it was an OK outcome.

The more important issue came late in the bike leg, when the first real red flags of the day started to appear. Not cardiovascular red flags, thankfully, but cramps. Both lower legs started to threaten, which is never a great sign when you still have another run to do.

Run 2

I did get out onto the second run, but from the first steps it was obvious that this was no longer about racing hard.

I had to keep the effort very controlled because even a small increase seemed to trigger cramps. And it was not just the calves. I was getting cramping in both lower and upper legs, and even between the shoulders.

So the final run became an exercise in damage limitation. Keep moving, stay just below the point where everything locks up, and get to the finish line without the whole thing turning into a shuffle.

With about 500m to go, one guy in my age group came past me. Part of me wanted to go with him, just to see if I could hold on. But I was too aware of what the legs were threatening to do. Chasing him might have meant blowing up completely, and that did not feel worth it on a day like this. On top of that, his calf nuber was 46: he was in my age group for the race, but not for the qualification spot at the World Championship.

So I let him go and focused on bringing it home cleanly.

Performance review

Results

And then, finally, the finish line.

I was not expecting much from this race, so ending up 2nd in category felt better than I had any real right to expect. It was a bit bittersweet, because I do think that on a better day I could probably have won this one.

Considering everything that happened in the lead-in to this race, I happily take this result.

This race was also the last World Championship qualifier of the year, which adds another layer to it.

Maybe this was enough to secure the spot, if I had not already done enough earlier in the season. But qualification is never that simple. There were five guys in the 30-39 range who were faster than me, and if any of them submitted an EOI and age into 40-44 next year, they would move ahead in the official federation ranking. So for now it is still a waiting game. We should know more in the next few weeks.

There is also the season ranking side of it. This result should put me 2nd in the Multisport Canada duathlon standings, level on points with the guy who finished ahead of me today, and 25 points behind the leader, who now has two wins from two races. So there is still a realistic chance of finishing the season in the top three.
